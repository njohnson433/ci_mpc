CI-MPC Assembly Pipeline

How a part gets from the gripper into a hole, built in stages: each stage switches on one part of the pipeline and keeps the rest simplified. The current focus is stage B, making the contact-implicit MPC work. The goal is a controller that generalizes across assembly tasks, not one tuned to the 8 mm peg.

The insertion pipeline, step by step

Contact time covers steps 4 to 6. Almost all of it is the hole search in step 5; the slide into the hole takes under 1 s (median 0.8 s on D1). With a 2 mm camera error, 88 % of starts are more than 1 mm off, so the search is needed. The plain push went in 12/12 at ≤ 1 mm offset on H5, but only 3/12 at ≥ 2 mm.

1

Grasp the part

simplified: known pose (stage A)

The Franka Hand closes on the standing part. For now the controller gets the true in-hand pose from the sim (--known-grasp).

Why simplifyThe grasp tips the part ~0.4°, leaving it 0.2–0.4 mm off centre. That is larger than many clearances. Assuming a known pose lets us work on the contact controller first.
Later (stage D)Estimate the in-hand pose once after grasping. Gripper width and the parallel-jaw constraint make that a small problem.
2

Transport in free space (MPC stage 1)

works on real FR3

The MPC moves the part above the camera's hole estimate while the impedance controller tracks it.

StatusReal FR3: steps ≤ 0.4 mm. Circle 1.9 mm RMS without friction compensation, 0.52–0.62 mm with velocity compensation.
3

Grasp-height calibration

dropped in stage A

It used to touch the top face once to learn how far the part sticks out of the hand. With a known grasp, the tip height comes directly from the in-hand pose.

4

Smooth approach and contact detection

building (stage A)

Decelerate toward the surface height expected from the camera. Detect contact when the force observer reads Fz > 1 N (--smooth-approach).

Why it changedThe old rule descended at full speed (10 mm/s) and declared contact once the command was 3 mm below the stalled tip (1.5 N spring force). It works, but it hits the surface hard. The observer is now validated to ±0.2 N, so it can detect contact directly.
New metricSpeed at contact and peak jerk are logged, so smoothness counts in the comparison.
5

Find the hole: camera prior + likelihood search

integrating (stage A)

The camera's estimate becomes a Gaussian prior with a 2 mm spread (--prior-sigma 0.002). Each probe presses at a point, reads tip height and force, and updates the belief through the learned likelihood. The next probe goes where it is most informative.

What the likelihood isA learned model of how likely each probe reading is if the hole is at a given position. It turns probe readings into a sharper belief about the hole position.
Which model
  • v5-clamp: trained only on batched-sim probes. Ties v3 in closed loop (R: 23.9 vs 23.1 s) and is robust to the real arm's force-estimate error (1.3 vs 3.2 mm for v3). Candidate.
  • Peg only. Other parts use the hand-built model; sharp-edged holes use the chamferless search (H5 21/24 vs 15/24).
6

Insert: plain push (A) → CI-MPC (B)

plain push = baseline CI-MPC: B1

Once the tip is in the bore and the belief is tight, the controller drives the part to the bottom. Stage A uses the plain push (push-stop tolerance under test). Stage B replaces it with the new CI-MPC.

Why the old C3 failed
  • It took over too early, mid-search, aiming at an estimate up to 4 mm off (clearance 0.51 mm).
  • Hand-built linearized contact model with a frozen contact set.
  • Arm friction model far from Isaac.
  • 100–700 ms per decision.
  • It optimized pose-to-goal, so it acted as a slower plain push.
New CI-MPC (approved design)
  • Analytic SDF of primitives (cylinder/box from dimensions, rounded edges): gap and normal everywhere, no CAD.
  • Smoothed complementarity: softplus normal force, regularized Coulomb friction.
  • LM over 5–10 steps at 20–50 Hz, warm start, ≤ 10 ms.
  • Objective: normal force 3–5 N, lateral and torque caps, axis alignment, steady progress that slows near the bottom, smooth commands.
  • Hole estimate refined from contacts every tick, starting from the search's belief.
7

Seat and check success

adopted

Success: within 1.0 mm of full depth and 1.5 mm in xy, from sim truth. We report three wrist-force numbers: the max (often a single 17 ms seating spike, up to 25 N), a 50 ms peak, and the max before seating.

What runs underneath every step

Impedance controllercartesian_impedance_control

Cartesian spring-damper on the FR3, runs inside Isaac's physics step in sim. Friction compensation lives here. hybrid = real-robot default velocity = Isaac default

Force observerexternal wrench estimate

In sim it matches true wrist force (−0.06 ± 0.20 N). On the real arm, Franka's estimate reads 0.13–0.37 of a friction-held force as external force. Modeled in sim, per-joint refit pending real logs.

Full sim pipelineIsaac + ROS 2 control

The real controller, planner and probing logic, one episode per Isaac instance. This is the benchmark ground truth, with a simulated wrist F/T (log lag 0.235 s, corrected by wrist_csv.py).

Batched sim256–512 parallel envs

Fast probe sim for likelihood training data. Matched to the full pipeline at 5.0 N press (agreement 0.66, above the pipeline's 0.47–0.50 with itself).

Arm scenesfriction models

Nominal: about 2× stickier than the real arm. realfric matches the real breakaway and stands in for the real robot.
